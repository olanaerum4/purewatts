What to Buy
Cheap
and What Not To

Not everything from Temu or AliExpress is worth it. Some things are genuinely identical to branded gear. Others will get you hurt. Here's the breakdown — no fluff.

I've been testing budget gear from Temu and AliExpress for a while now, and the honest truth is this: about 80% of cycling and running accessories can be bought cheap without any meaningful performance difference. Socks are socks. Bar tape is bar tape. A bottle cage either holds the bottle or it doesn't.

But there are categories where buying cheap is genuinely dangerous — and a few where it's just a bad investment that'll cost you more in the long run. This guide is my honest take on where the line is.

✅ Always Worth Buying Cheap

These are categories where the performance difference between budget and branded is either zero or close enough that it doesn't matter. Buy cheap, buy in bulk, replace when worn out.

Cycling Socks
A sock's job is to wick sweat and not bunch up. Budget socks do this identically to $30 Castelli pairs. Buy five and rotate — that's still cheaper than one branded pair.

⚠️ Worth It — With Caveats

These categories can be great value, but you need to read the reviews carefully and know the limitations before you buy.

Bib Shorts
The shorts themselves are fine — the chamois (pad) quality is the variable. Budget bibs can work well for 2–3 hour rides. For 5+ hours, a bad chamois will ruin your day. Check reviews specifically for long-ride comfort, and test on training rides before a race. The YKYWBIKE bibs on this list have strong reviews for this.
Test first
Gloves (all types)
Budget gloves are generally excellent value, but Asian sizing runs small consistently. If you order your normal size you'll probably find them tight. Size up by one and you're usually fine. The padding and grip are legitimately good once you nail the fit.
Size up
Mini Pumps
Perfect for roadside emergencies and getting home. Not ideal for precise race pressure — max PSI ratings on budget pumps are optimistic, and getting to true road pressures takes real effort. Pair one with a CO2 inflator for best results. For training rides, they're completely fine.
Emergency use
Sunglasses
UV400 is claimed on budget lenses, but third-party lab certification is rare. For casual riding in moderate sun, fine. For 6-hour summer rides in intense UV, the brand-name lenses with verified certification are worth the investment — eyes are not something to gamble with on long outdoor exposure.
Check UV cert
Cycling Jerseys & Gilets
The fabrics and fit are generally very good, but sizing consistency varies between brands. Check measurements carefully rather than trusting S/M/L. When reviews say "runs small" take it seriously. Once you find one that fits, the quality-to-price ratio is excellent.
Check sizing
Carbon-Look Components (non-structural)
Carbon-look ABS bottle cages, handlebar tape, and cosmetic parts are totally fine. But verify what you're buying — the bottle cage on this list is honest about being ABS. Some listings claim "carbon fiber" when it isn't. ABS is fine for cages. Just know what you're getting.

❌ Never Cheap Out On These

This is the short list. These are either safety-critical, performance-critical, or items where the cost of getting it wrong is much higher than the money saved.

Helmets
Non-negotiable. Certified helmets from established brands (Giro, Bell, POC, Lazer) undergo rigorous impact testing to EN1078, CPSC, or MIPS standards. Off-brand helmets may claim certification but it's unverifiable. Your head is not a place to save $30. Buy a certified helmet, full stop.
Never cheap
Brake Components
Brake pads, rotors, hydraulic lines, and calipers are safety-critical. A budget brake pad that glazes, a rotor that warps, or a hydraulic line that fails is a crash waiting to happen. Use known brands for anything in your braking system. This is not an area for experimentation.
Never cheap
Carbon Frames, Forks & Stems
Counterfeit carbon is one of the most dangerous things in cycling. A carbon frame that fails at 45km/h downhill can kill you. Forks and stems too. Only buy structural carbon components from brands with real quality control and crash replacement policies. The savings are not worth the risk.
Never cheap
Power Meters
A power meter's entire value is accuracy. If it reads 10% high, every training zone you've built your fitness around is wrong. Budget power meters exist, but accuracy testing is hard to verify. Stick to Garmin, Stages, Favero, 4iiii, or similar brands with published accuracy specs.
Never cheap
Bike Shoes
Fit precision matters here in a way it doesn't for most clothing. Wrong fit causes hot spots, pressure points, and over time — knee and hip injuries. Cleat alignment is also critical. Budget shoes can work, but buy from a brand you can return to, try them on if possible, and don't be surprised if you need to spend more to get the fit right.
Spend the money
Chain
This one surprises people. A cheap chain wears out faster — and a worn chain accelerates wear on your cassette and chainrings, which are much more expensive to replace. Shimano, KMC, or SRAM chains are $20–35 and last much longer. A false economy to go cheaper.
False economy
The 80/20 rule for kit spending: About 80% of what you wear and carry on a bike or run can be bought cheap without compromise. Spend that saved money on the 20% that genuinely matters — good shoes, a certified helmet, a quality drivetrain. That's how you go faster without going broke.
